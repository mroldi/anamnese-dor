◉ Background & Rationale

Low back pain (LBP) is the most frequent pain complaint worldwide and the leading cause of years lived with disability (YLDs) globally, with global YLDs increasing 52.7% between 1990 and 2017 to reach 64.9 million.3,4,5

Approximately 90% of cases are considered non-specific, lacking a clear pathoanatomical cause.1,2 Contemporary physiopathology of LBP relies on a biopsychosocial model, integrating biological, psychological, and social factors — superseding the traditional injury-based model.1,2

Under the current IASP pain classification, LBP may present as nociceptive, neuropathic, nociplastic, or mixed pain.2 Myofascial pain syndrome (MPS), a primary nociceptive condition, may coexist with neuropathic or nociplastic components, complicating diagnosis.6,7

⚙ Objectives

To characterize the clinical and diagnostic profile of patients with chronic LBP referred from the Spinal Unit to an Interdisciplinary Pain Clinic, identifying pain mechanisms, comorbidities, and prior treatment patterns.

🔬 Methods

Design: Retrospective observational study. Review of medical records.

Period: January 2025 – December 2025

Setting: Interdisciplinary Pain Clinic, Institute of Orthopedics, quaternary teaching hospital, São Paulo, Brazil.

Population: Consecutive patients referred from the Spinal Unit with the diagnosis of chronic LBP.

Variables collected: Demographics (sex, age), pain duration (months), previous lumbar surgery, pain type classification, myofascial syndrome, sleep quality, physical activity, mood, and prior pharmacological treatments.

Diagnostic approach: Detailed history plus comprehensive musculoskeletal and neurological examination performed in all patients.

★ Main Findings Summary LBP was a primary reason for referral to the orthopedic pain clinic. The high prevalence of MPS (100%), non-restorative sleep (75%), physical inactivity (77%), mood impairment (100%), prior surgeries (38%), and opioid use (83%) highlights the multifactorial, biopsychosocial nature of this population.
✔ Conclusions
  • MPS was the main physical abnormality in all LBP patients, confirming its central role in the orthopedic pain clinic population.
  • Nociceptive/nociplastic sources of pain were more frequent (69%) than mixed nociceptive-neuropathic pain (31%).
  • The very high prevalence of opioid use (83%) prior to pain clinic referral indicates a treatment mismatch with the predominant musculoskeletal pain mechanism.
  • The convergence of non-restorative sleep, physical inactivity, and mood impairment in all patients reinforces the recommendation for a multidisciplinary, mechanism-oriented treatment approach.
