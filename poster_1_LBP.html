Low Back Pain in an Orthopedic Pain Clinic: Diagnosis Challenges

1. Introduction

Low back pain (LBP) is the leading cause of years lived with disability (YLDs) globally, with an estimated 577 million prevalent cases and 64.9 million YLDs reported in 2017 — representing a 52.7% increase since 1990.3,4,5 Approximately 90% of cases are classified as non-specific, lacking a clearly identifiable pathoanatomical cause.1,2

Contemporary physiopathology of LBP operates within a biopsychosocial framework that integrates biological, psychological, and social determinants, superseding the traditional injury-based paradigm.1,2 Under the current IASP taxonomy, LBP may manifest as nociceptive, neuropathic, nociplastic, or mixed pain, each requiring distinct therapeutic strategies.2

Myofascial pain syndrome (MPS) — a primary nociceptive condition characterised by myofascial trigger points (MTrPs) — is frequently under-recognised as a co-occurring diagnosis in patients with LBP referred from surgical subspecialties.6,7 Its identification has direct therapeutic implications, given that MPS management diverges substantially from structural or purely pharmacological approaches.

2. Objectives

To characterise the clinical and diagnostic profile of patients with chronic LBP referred from the Spinal Unit to an Interdisciplinary Pain Clinic (IPC), with emphasis on:

(i) pain mechanism classification (nociceptive, nociplastic, neuropathic, mixed); (ii) prevalence and role of MPS; (iii) prior pharmacological treatment patterns; and (iv) biopsychosocial comorbidities.

3. Methods
Design Retrospective, single-centre observational study
Setting IPC, Institute of Orthopedics and Traumatology (IOT), HCFMUSP — quaternary-level public teaching hospital, São Paulo, Brazil
Period January–December 2025
Population Consecutive patients referred from the Spinal Unit to the IPC with a primary diagnosis of chronic LBP
Variables Sociodemographic data; pain duration; pain type (nociceptive / neuropathic / nociplastic / mixed); concurrent diagnoses; pharmacological history; sleep quality; physical activity; mood status
Assessment Standardised comprehensive musculoskeletal and neurological examination by the interdisciplinary pain team in all patients

Key Finding The convergence of universal MPS (100%), near-universal mood impairment (100%), physical inactivity (77.8%), non-restorative sleep (75.0%), and high opioid burden (83.3%) confirms the profound biopsychosocial complexity of chronic LBP in this quaternary orthopedic referral population, and underscores the inadequacy of single-mechanism pharmacological approaches.
5. Conclusions
  • 1 LBP was a leading indication for referral from the Spinal Unit to the Interdisciplinary Pain Clinic.
  • 2 MPS was identified in all evaluated patients (100%), constituting the primary musculoskeletal abnormality regardless of overall pain type.
  • 3 Nociceptive and nociplastic mechanisms predominated (69%), with mixed musculoskeletal-neuropathic pain accounting for 31% of cases.
  • 4 Opioid use prior to pain clinic referral (83.3%) indicates treatment mismatch relative to the predominant musculoskeletal pain mechanism.
  • 5 The biopsychosocial burden observed reinforces the imperative for a multidisciplinary, mechanism-oriented therapeutic approach in this population.
