Low Back Pain in an Orthopedic Pain Clinic: Diagnosis Challenges

Azeredo MAR • Sperandio RW • Santos RG • Ramos DFRH • Pereira RJ • Fernandes AM • Pacini L • Sena R • Iobbi JA • Panteon LG • Yonekawa NS • Kubota G • Kaziyama HH • Sabe FF • Andrade DC • Teixeira MJ • Yeng LT

Study cohort 18 Patients
evaluated
1. Introduction

Low back pain (LBP) is the leading cause of years lived with disability (YLDs) globally, with an estimated 577 million prevalent cases and 64.9 million YLDs reported in 2017 — representing a 52.7% increase since 1990.3,4,5 Approximately 90% of cases are classified as non-specific, lacking a clearly identifiable pathoanatomical cause.1,2

Contemporary physiopathology of LBP operates within a biopsychosocial framework integrating biological, psychological, and social determinants.1,2 Under the current IASP taxonomy, LBP may manifest as nociceptive, neuropathic, nociplastic, or mixed pain, each requiring distinct therapeutic strategies.2

Myofascial pain syndrome (MPS) — characterised by myofascial trigger points (MTrPs) — is frequently under-recognised as a co-occurring diagnosis in LBP patients referred from surgical subspecialties.6,7 Its identification carries direct therapeutic implications.

2. Objectives

To characterise the clinical and diagnostic profile of patients with chronic LBP referred from the Spinal Unit to an Interdisciplinary Pain Clinic (IPC), with emphasis on:

(i) pain mechanism classification (nociceptive, nociplastic, neuropathic, mixed); (ii) prevalence and role of MPS; (iii) prior pharmacological treatment patterns; and (iv) biopsychosocial comorbidities.

3. Methods
Design Retrospective, single-centre observational study
Setting IPC, IOT, HCFMUSP — quaternary-level public teaching hospital, São Paulo, Brazil
Period January–December 2025
Population Consecutive patients referred from the Spinal Unit to the IPC with chronic LBP
Variables Sociodemographic data; pain duration; pain type; concurrent diagnoses; pharmacological history; sleep; physical activity; mood
Assessment Standardised comprehensive musculoskeletal and neurological examination by the interdisciplinary pain team
MPS Criteria MPS diagnosed by systematic palpation per Simons, Travell & Simons (1999): (i) palpable taut band; (ii) hypersensitive trigger point (MTrP) within the band; (iii) reproduction of the patient's familiar pain on compression; (iv) characteristic referred pain pattern. Active and latent MTrPs distinguished. Consistent with IASP nociceptive pain classification.
Statistics Descriptive statistics only (frequencies, means). No inferential analysis — exploratory single-centre design.

★ Key Finding The convergence of universal MPS (100%), mood impairment (100%), physical inactivity (77.8%), non-restorative sleep (75.0%), and high opioid burden (83.3%) confirms the profound biopsychosocial complexity of chronic LBP in this quaternary orthopedic referral population, and underscores the inadequacy of single-mechanism pharmacological approaches.
5. Conclusions
  • 1 LBP was a leading indication for referral from the Spinal Unit to the Interdisciplinary Pain Clinic.
  • 2 MPS was identified in all patients (100%), constituting the primary musculoskeletal abnormality regardless of overall pain type.
  • 3 Nociceptive/nociplastic mechanisms predominated (69%), with mixed pain in 31%.
  • 4 Opioid use prior to referral (83.3%) indicates treatment mismatch relative to the predominant musculoskeletal mechanism.
  • 5 Biopsychosocial burden reinforces the need for a multidisciplinary, mechanism-oriented therapeutic approach.
Limitations

Sample size: n = 18 limits statistical precision; each patient represents ≈5.6% of the cohort. Findings should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating.

Design: Retrospective, single-centre; findings are not generalisable to primary care or non-specialised LBP populations.

Selection: Quaternary-referral context; prevalence of MPS, prior opioid use, and biopsychosocial burden likely exceeds community estimates.
